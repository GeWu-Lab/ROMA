Overview

Sensing that goes and gets what it needs

Humans understand the physical world through an active process. When sensory evidence is insufficient, we interact: we lift an object to feel its weight, or shake it to hear what is inside. Existing multi-sensory robot systems mostly integrate the inputs they are given, rather than acquire the evidence they are missing.

ROMA is an LLM-based system that closes this gap. It integrates vision, audio, tactile and force sensing into a reasoning–interaction–feedback loop: the model identifies missing evidence and chooses the target object, interaction and modality, while a physical interface executes it and returns the multi-sensory feedback.

Dataset

ROMI-2K

Real-world object–interaction data with synchronized vision, audio, touch and force, for handheld objects and robot tabletop scenes.

 System

ROMA-7B

A multi-sensory LLM trained in two stages, paired with a grasp-and-interact physical interface that runs on a real robot arm.

 Benchmark

ROMA Bench

Perception chains for single-attribute, long-horizon multi-attribute and intent-driven active perception.

ROMI-2K

Real-world object interaction, at object scale

Existing datasets trade object diversity against interaction diversity. ROMI-2K covers nearly 2,000 everyday objects, each with six atomic interactions and synchronized multi-sensory feedback, collected two complementary ways.

Handheld collection

1,657 object–content combinations

A modified multi-sensory UMI (Pika Sense with a microphone and two GelSight Mini sensors). Every object is handled at 3 grasp locations, and container contents are varied to capture diverse audio.

  • Wrist + third view
  • Audio
  • Touch × 2
Tabletop scenes

500 scenes, 1–5 objects each

An xArm 6 with a Robotiq 2F-140 gripper grasps each object and runs all six interactions. 400 scenes for training and 100 for test, including 56 unseen objects.

  • RGB + depth
  • Audio
  • Touch × 2
  • 6-axis force

Dataset statistics

Object materials

Objects with contents inside

Every object is annotated (Gemini 3.5 Flash, then manually verified) with identity, material, hardness, roughness, texture, contents, and the sensory modality that can reveal them.

Method

A reasoning–interaction–feedback loop

Given an instruction and an initial scene, ROMA decides whether the current evidence is enough. If not, it names the object, the interaction and the modalities to read, then reasons over what comes back, until it can answer.

1

Reason

The Multi-Sensory LLM (ROMA-7B) finds what is missing, then picks the target object, action token and modalities.
2

Interact

A grasp interface and six atomic interactions turn the decision into robot motion.
3

Sense

Vision, audio, touch and force are recorded, encoded and returned for the next decision.

until the evidence is sufficient → answer

1

Reason

ROMA-7B, built on Qwen2.5-Omni, learns to judge sufficiency and choose what to sense next.

Tokens

Action & modality tokens

Six action tokens, <grasp_start>/<grasp_end> around a bounding box, and positional tokens for touch and force. Each is initialized from the averaged embeddings of its words plus Gaussian noise.

Stage 1

Multi-sensory alignment

Audio and touch are aligned to the vision–text space with audio-text, audio-visual-text, tactile-text and tactile-visual-text instruction tuning, so ambiguous signals are grounded in object semantics.

Stage 2

Active perception SFT

The model analyzes the scene, decides whether evidence is sufficient, and otherwise emits the next object, interaction and modalities. It then reasons over the returned feedback and repeats.

Dynamic scene sampling

A smooth shift from touching things to solving scenes

Handheld and pseudo-scene samples draw positions from Beta(1, α), real-scene samples from Beta(α, 1), with α = 2.5. Sorting by position front-loads interaction-level supervision and leaves realistic scenes, and the force modality, for late training.

2

Interact

A training-free grasp policy plus six pre-defined interactions form the interface between the LLM and the robot.

Grasp interface

Stable contact for sensing

AnyGrasp proposes grasps from the object’s point cloud. A symmetry-based completion recovers geometry that transparent and reflective objects lose, then candidates are filtered by height, reachability and orientation, and refined for our gripper.

Six atomic interactions

  • <lift>Raise the object and hold it
  • <press>Press it down against the table
  • <collide>Tap it on the table, repeatedly
  • <shake>Shake it back and forth
  • <rotate>Turn it 180° about the grasp axis
  • <squeeze>Close the gripper a little further
3

Sense

Every interaction is recorded by four modalities; the model chooses which of them to read.

Vision

One wrist-camera image per interaction; the initial scene comes from RGB-D views.

Audio

One cropped microphone segment, most informative for <shake> and <rotate>.

Touch

Two GelSight Mini images, one at the start and one during the interaction.

Force

A wrist force sensor. The mean gravity component is given to the LLM as text.

Raw force is moved to the robot base frame and the empty-gripper force is removed: Ftrans = RB←G RG←S (Fraw − Fraw0)

ROMA Bench

Perception chains

Active perception unfolds as a chain: each interaction gives new evidence that shapes the next one. ROMA Bench holds 2,100 scene-level tasks from the tabletop test split, in three flavors.

Single-chain

“Which objects are empty?”

One target attribute, one chain of interactions.

Multi-chain

“Is the heaviest object also empty?”

Several attributes. Chains share feedback, so redundant interactions should be skipped.

Intent-driven

“Find me a treat to eat.”

The model infers which attributes matter from an implicit goal, then chains them.

Tasks by type

Question formats

  • Multiple choice
  • True or false
  • Ranking
  • 100 held-out scenes with 56 unseen objects
  • 132 free-form tasks re-created in 8 real scenes
  • Intent-driven questions written from scene annotations

Which attributes appear together

Arc length: tasks involving the attribute (Single + Multi-Chain). Ribbons: attribute pairs co-occurring in Multi-Chain tasks; ribbon widths are illustrative.

A predicted grasp box must overlap a ground-truth object by at least 70%, otherwise the task fails. All test objects are disjoint from the training and handheld data.

    Analysis

    How ROMA interacts, and why each sense matters

    Accuracy vs. interaction cost

    Interactions per scene on ROMA Bench with oracle boxes. Exhaustive grasps every object and runs all six interactions, about 4× the cost of ROMA-7B. GPT-5.4 and Gemini stop earlier, but often before the evidence is sufficient.

    Training ablations

    Drop in overall success (72.9%) when one component is removed.

    Share of each interaction

      Frontier models lean on <lift> and <squeeze>. ROMA uses <rotate>, <press> and <collide> as complementary probes.

      Take a sense away, lose the matching skill

      Single-chain success when the modality returns “Not Available”. Overall: 72.9 → 64.1 without audio, 62.7 without touch, 62.2 without force.

      A case study

      Question: Which of the three rightmost objects are empty?

      GT: the white box

      GPT-5.4

      ✕ the grey cube

      <grasp> white box on the left <\grasp> (use touch and force) <shake>. The grey block is not a container. <grasp> black box on the right <\grasp> (use touch and force) <squeeze>. [the grey cube]

      • Inappropriate action–modality pairs
      • Lack of active interaction due to overconfidence

      Gemini 3.5 Flash

      ✕ the grey cube

      I need to interact with each of them (the white box, the grey cube, and the black box) by shaking them to hear if there is anything inside. <grasp> <\grasp> (use audio and force) <shake>. <grasp> <\grasp> (use audio and force) <shake>. <grasp> <\grasp> (use audio and force) <shake>. The answer is [the grey cube].

      • Inappropriate action–modality pairs

      ROMA-7B

      ✓ the white box

      <grasp> the white box <\grasp> (use audio) <shake> (use audio) <rotate>. … little noise. (use vision, touch and force) <squeeze>. The mean gravity force … similar to an empty version. <grasp> the grey block <\grasp> (use audio) <shake> The object produces … when shaken. <grasp> the black box <\grasp> (use audio) <shake>. The object produces … when shaken. The answer is the white box.

      • Continues interacting when evidence is insufficient
      • Selects complementary actions and modalities

      GPT-5.4 tends to stop with fewer interactions, occasionally skipping critical objects and sensory evidence. GPT-5.4 and Gemini 3.5 Flash also pair actions and modalities badly, for example trying to read gravity force during vigorous shaking.
